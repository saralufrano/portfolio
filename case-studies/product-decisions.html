CASE STUDY 03 / PRODUCT JUDGMENT

Deciding where to spend the effort.

Three decisions about engineering investment: build the core platform, buy a solution for a deadline, and retire a capability with recurring operational costs.

01 / BUILD THE CORE

Invest where control and fit matter.

The legacy codebase made even small updates expensive and slow. I evaluated retrofitting it, buying Ventrata, and building an internal platform.

Our assessment considered development cost, attraction-specific requirements, control of the roadmap, and the cost of connecting distribution partners. An internal build let the team prioritize the organization’s needs directly and build the partner connections it needed.

The decision was to invest in a core platform the business could operate and evolve, including product, pricing, inventory, permissions, and channel capabilities.

02 / BUY THE DEADLINE

Protect the roadmap without losing the opportunity.

Marketing wanted a season pass for Costco, the website, and box office in about a month. Requirements expanded to include partner discounts and additional attraction benefits. The internal distribution and configuration capabilities needed for that launch were not ready.

At the same time, engineering was finishing foundational capabilities required for rules-based dynamic pricing.

I chose JoinIt. Even if we could have matched its cost internally, we could not match its delivery timing without pulling capacity from the larger platform roadmap.

The tradeoff was capacity and timing.

The season-pass solution was delivered within the month, while the core team stayed focused on the dynamic-pricing prerequisites.

03 / RETIRE THE RECURRING WORK

Let behavior change the product.

After we introduced QR ticket delivery, printing was still used roughly half the time. The printers also generated recurring work for Operations and IT: refills, adjustments, failures, and restarts.

I weighed that usage against the support burden and stopped supporting kiosk printing. Guests who want a paper ticket can have it reprinted at box office.

The digital approach continued with the replacement kiosks. A paper option remained available without requiring a printer at every kiosk.

04 / THE PRINCIPLE

Engineering time should serve the business goal.

These were different decisions made for different constraints. The core platform justified investment in fit and control. The season pass needed a solution within a commercial deadline. Printing required reconsideration after we saw how the experience worked in practice.

My role was to make those tradeoffs explicit and give the team a clear path forward.
