01 / THE BUSINESS PROBLEM

“We want to change the price.”

That was the visible problem when I joined. Price changes took about two weeks. Routine product ideas could take three to four weeks to reach a deployment, and pricing, product setup, and inventory depended on specialized knowledge and developer involvement.

The combined business recorded 2.4 million tickets and $83.9 million in sales, excluding tax, in 2025. Giving its operators more control was a commercial priority.

02 / THE PRODUCT DECISION

Pricing first. The foundations to make it possible.

I prioritized pricing flexibility against the business’s revenue objective. A different objective, such as improving NPS, would have led to a different starting point.

Instant price changes were the first capability I designed and planned for Control Station. Getting to flexible and eventually rules-based pricing required a product model, inventory controls, permissions, discounts, bundles, and sales-channel configuration.

I defined that roadmap and sequence, then made the case for an additional $200K in 2025 capital, roughly doubling the year’s capital allocation. That investment funded the prerequisites for the spring 2026 rules-based dynamic pricing launch.

Strategy became an investment sequence.

The platform had approximately $1.8M in combined capital and operating funding across 2025–2026. The roadmap connected that investment to the capabilities the business needed next.

03 / DELIVERY AND ADOPTION

A platform replacement is an organizational change.

I led product direction with a team spanning backend, frontend, design, QA, business analysis, and project management. The work connected administrative capabilities to new web, kiosk, and box office experiences.

  1. Build the foundationsPricing, product setup, inventory, permissions, discounts, bundles, and sales-channel controls.
  2. Complete the cutover · January 2026Move both attractions’ direct selling channels to the new platform and retire the legacy ticketing software.
  3. Enable the next commercial capabilities · Spring 2026Launch rules-based dynamic pricing and upsell/promotion capabilities.

Weekly demos, user training, documentation, and readiness checks with department heads and executives prepared the organization to switch. The cutover ran around 4 a.m. while box office and kiosks were closed. Web was unavailable for a few hours during the banking integration change.

04 / WHAT CHANGED

The business could act directly.

  • Authorized users can publish routine products immediately and change prices in real time.
  • The platform removed the need for a dedicated ticketing administrator. That position could focus on product and IT work instead.
  • Product coordination meetings and manual handoffs went away.
  • Operators gained visibility into selling time slots and the ability to adjust inventory in real time.
  • Upsell and promotion capabilities supported $106K in recorded 2026 YTD sales.

As demand softened, I adapted priorities toward flexible discounts, coupons, and product configuration. The capabilities built into the platform gave the business more ways to respond.
